🩸 Cycle — Reproductive & Menstrual Health

Menstrual health is more than a monthly inconvenience. It's a window into systemic health, mental state, and quality of life — and many conditions remain dramatically underfunded.

  • PMDD: Affects 3-8% of people who menstruate. Cyclical, severe emotional and physical symptoms during the luteal phase. Frequently misdiagnosed as bipolar disorder or generalized depression.
  • Endometriosis: Affects 1 in 10 people who menstruate. Average time to diagnosis: 7 to 10 years. Pain often dismissed as "bad periods" by clinicians.
  • Adenomyosis: The uterine lining grows into the muscle wall. Causes heavy bleeding, severe cramping, and chronic pelvic pain. Heavily underresearched.

📓 Psyche — Mental Health & Self-Reflection

The link between hormonal fluctuations and mental health is real, documented, and still largely absent from how we teach college students to understand their own minds.

Cycle phases:

  • Menstrual: Rest, reflection, lower energy. Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest.
  • Follicular: Rising estrogen = sharper cognition, more social energy, better mood.
  • Ovulatory: Peak estrogen and LH surge. Many people report highest confidence and communication clarity.
  • Luteal: Progesterone rises, then crashes. Anxiety, irritability, and sleep disruption are common. PMDD symptoms peak here.

The Psyche & Cycle Podcast covers these intersections in depth. Find us on Spotify.

Our Focus

What We Do

  1. Mental Health Education

    Research-backed resources and open conversations to destigmatize the emotional impact of hormonal fluctuations. We teach the cycle as a system, not a symptom.

  2. Community & Safe Space

    Student-led spaces where shared experiences become the curriculum. We know isolation is part of chronic health struggles. We build the opposite.

  3. Menstrual Rights Advocacy

    Fighting period poverty and pushing for systemic change in access to menstrual care. Because health equity starts with the basics.

What We've Done

Our Work

  • Period Product Drive

    200+ menstrual products collected and distributed via I Support the Girls. Directly fighting period poverty in our local community.

  • PA HOSA State Conference

    1st place in Mental Health Promotion among 3,000+ students statewide. Our campaign linked menstrual and mental health in documented, measurable ways.

  • HOSA International (Houston)

    5th place among 85 international teams. We brought our research and community data to a national audience and left with more conviction than when we arrived.

    During Mental Health Awareness Month, we filled our campus with handwritten notes. Small, human, and something people actually remembered.

  • PMDD Research Study

    Three educational sessions with pre/post surveys measuring shifts in awareness and stigma. The results showed real change.

  • Yoga for Cramping Relief

    A therapeutic group session pairing breathwork and gentle stretches with clinical context. Not just wellness — education with a yoga mat.

Shedding Light

Underresearched Disorders

PMDD — 3–8% of people who menstruate

Unlike typical PMS, PMDD is a severe, chronic condition with emotional and physical symptoms that peak during the luteal phase. It is frequently misdiagnosed as bipolar disorder or depression because clinicians don't track cycles.

IAPMD.org

Endometriosis — 1 in 10 people who menstruate

Tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus. The average time to diagnosis is 7 to 10 years. Pain is routinely dismissed by clinicians as normal menstrual discomfort.

Endofound.org

Adenomyosis — Severely underresearched

The uterine lining grows into the muscle wall itself, causing heavy bleeding, severe cramping, and chronic pelvic pain. Many people with adenomyosis are told their symptoms are psychosomatic.

Who We Are

About Us

"The purpose of this club is primarily to promote education on the relationship between mental health and the menstrual cycle."

Psyche & Cycle was founded by students who noticed a gap that felt both obvious and invisible: mental health and menstrual health were discussed in entirely separate rooms, despite their documented biological connection.

We are a student-led advocacy organization working to dismantle the stigma around both periods and mental illness. Through education, direct advocacy, and community events, we aim to give students the language and the support systems to understand their bodies and minds as one system.

We operate across three campuses and are always looking for people who want to help build something real.
